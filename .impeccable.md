## Design Context

Current brand direction for Jay Lawrence Dimaano's portfolio, updated September 15, 2026.

### Users

The primary audience is time-constrained hiring decision-makers: founders and CTOs, engineering leads, product and design leads, and recruiters. They need to establish Jay's relevance quickly, verify that his work and ownership are credible, inspect one or two projects for depth, and decide whether to open the resume or start a conversation. The experience must support both a 30-second scan and a detailed technical audit.

### Brand Personality

Quiet confidence, precise craft, and a candid human voice. Jay's positioning centers on taste, judgment, and standards: deciding what is worth building, caring how it feels to use, and leaving maintainable software for the next person. AI is part of his workflow; he remains responsible for what ships.

Write in the first person with direct, conversational language. Allow brief, self-aware jokes about AI and occasional expressive details where they feel natural. The homepage's “Yes, I use AI too. I’m still picky about what ships.” is the voice reference. Keep professional claims specific and honest about ownership; humor should support the work, not replace its evidence.

### Aesthetic Direction

The primary reference is MONOLOG's flowing editorial storytelling: deliberate pacing, expressive type, asymmetric placement, and discoveries on interaction. Adapt those principles to an engineering portfolio with clear ownership and inspectable work. Author Jay's own composition and voice rather than reproducing the reference site's layout or assets.

Move readers through positioning, selected work, supporting depth, and a clear next action. Typography, real product imagery, generous space, and thin dividers carry the hierarchy. Both themes belong to the same identity: warm off-white in light mode and deep charcoal in dark mode.

- **Color:** keep neutrals dominant. Use neon lime `#CFFF04` for meaningful actions, proof highlights, and active details, with dark ink on lime fills. Use the darker semantic accent for readable light-theme text and focus treatment.
- **Type:** use Geist for headlines and body copy, with Geist Mono for short labels, dates, and metadata. Favor oversized, tightly tracked headlines at medium weight; keep body text comfortably spaced and readable.
- **Composition:** use shared alignment, intentional asymmetry, and open space. Let media expand when it makes the work easier to inspect. Keep the story out of tiled résumé dashboards, glowing card grids, and decorative gradients.

For brand artwork outside the website, carry over the neutral/lime palette, precise geometry, and generous negative space. Abstract software structures can express Jay's work; company or project evidence must use authentic media.

### Interaction Character

The resting interface is calm and immediately readable. Reward intentional hover, focus, and navigation with useful discoveries: alternate product views, company previews, and purposeful arrow reveals. Motion should reveal information and continuity, going beyond a generic scale effect without delaying reading.

Preserve native scrolling and the native cursor. A bounded pointer-following action marker may live inside project media. Keep transitions interruptible, keyboard changes immediate, and reduced-motion variants limited to short fades or immediate states. Touch retains direct navigation and access to the same essential evidence.

Use real product screenshots or company-published visuals for previews, with captions that distinguish Jay's contribution from the company's broader work. Keep preview placement, focus, dismissal, and click-through predictable.

Quiet interaction sounds are on by default, with an accessible mute control and a remembered visitor preference. Sound accompanies deliberate interaction, never continuous pointer movement; visual feedback must remain sufficient when playback is muted or unavailable.

### Design Principles

1. Make taste and judgment visible through decisions, ownership, and shipped outcomes.
2. Give every section a reason to follow the previous one.
3. Use emphasis and interaction to help readers understand or act.
4. Recompose hierarchy and media deliberately for mobile.
5. Treat accessibility, performance, and motion preferences as part of the craft.
