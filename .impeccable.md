## Design Context

### Users

The primary audience is time-constrained hiring decision-makers: founders and CTOs, engineering leads, product and design leads, and recruiters. They need to establish Jay's relevance quickly, verify that his work and ownership are credible, inspect one or two projects for depth, and decide whether to open the resume or start a conversation. The experience must support both a 30-second scan and a detailed technical audit.

### Brand Personality

Quiet, precise, and authored. The portfolio should communicate technical depth without developer theater, editorial clarity without coldness, and confident restraint with small moments of human warmth. Copy should be direct, evidence-led, and honest about scope or uncertainty.

### Aesthetic Direction

A refined editorial product journal with polished application behavior. Typography and evidence carry the interface; generous space, thin dividers, asymmetric composition, and restrained motion establish hierarchy. Both light and dark themes are supported. Neutrals remain dominant, with `#CFFF04` used strategically for calls to action, proof highlights, active details, and focus moments. Avoid generic black SaaS styling, tiled resume dashboards, glowing cards, decorative gradients, and motion that competes with reading.

### Design Principles

1. Put outcomes and ownership before supporting detail.
2. Use color to direct attention, never as decoration without meaning.
3. Keep the resting interface calm; reserve delight for moments of intent.
4. Preserve editorial scanning on desktop and deliberately recompose content for mobile.
5. Treat accessibility, performance, and reduced motion as visible aspects of quality.
